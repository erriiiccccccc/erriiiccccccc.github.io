04 · Lava Island · Experience

Make the work feel hot.

The timeline you have is clean, but nothing happens, so Lava ends up the calmest island in the world. Here are three ways to give it heat without going back to costume. Click into a scene to drive it.

Where it's at

src/islands/experience.js is a LinkedIn-style list: logo tile, role, org, year, one line. You picked it after the riveted-iron and minimal-spine versions felt cringe.

What's off

Every role has the same weight, so a spring internship looks as big as Barclays. Your best numbers (45 minutes, 6,000+) hide in small grey text. Nothing moves.

The move

Lead every role with its impact, and let heat mean recency. Newest glows, oldest has cooled. Light, not props.

Three ways to show experience

Pick a shape

All three use your 7 real roles from experience.js. Logos for Barclays, BlackRock and Edinburgh are the real files, the rest are monograms like today.

A · Strata

A cross-section of the island. Each era is a layer of rock, newest on top. Colour temperature encodes time: the top layer is molten, each older one has cooled a little more. The focused layer opens to show its impact.

Why it works

It's the only option that could only exist on a lava island, yet there's no costume: it's just light and layers. Heat as recency is a real encoding, not decoration, so the metaphor earns its place. One open layer at a time keeps focus.

HCI + accessibility

↑ ↓ moves through time, ← → between overlapping roles, click works too. Years are always written, so colour never carries meaning alone. The depth gauge shows where you are. Under reduced motion the molten flow stops.

How it'd be built

Group ENTRIES in experience.js into eras. A flex column where the open era gets a bigger flex-grow (animates for free). Temperature is one CSS variable per layer. Around 150 lines, no new assets.

Watch out

Only shines if each role has a real impact line. BlackRock, Outlier and Finexus are weak today. Past about 9 roles the collapsed layers get thin, so group older ones.

Why it works

Recruiters scan for outcomes, and this puts outcomes first. Size equals impact, so the hierarchy does the talking. Very modern, very professional.

HCI + accessibility

Tiles are buttons, Tab and Enter work. The expand uses the View Transitions API so the tile morphs instead of jumping, and falls back to instant. Text gradients keep solid contrast.

How it'd be built

Add an impact field and a size to each entry, a CSS grid with spans, document.startViewTransition for the expand. Around 100 lines.

Watch out

Bento grids are everywhere right now, so it's the least unique to Lava. Exposes weak numbers brutally. Loses the sense of time order.

Why it works

Shows hustle at a glance: overlapping bars say "did a lot at once" better than any sentence. The pulsing now line makes the panel feel alive. Data viz reads as engineering-minded.

HCI + accessibility

Bars are buttons with full labels, focus or hover shows the card. Type is shown by pattern and a legend, not colour alone. Fits without horizontal scrolling.

How it'd be built

Add start and end months to each entry, position bars by percentage. No library needed. Around 120 lines.

Watch out

Short internships become slivers that look tiny next to long side gigs, which undersells Barclays and BlackRock. Needs real months to be honest.

My pick

Strata, with Bento's impact-first copy inside each layer.

Strata is the one that makes Lava feel like Lava. It moves, it glows, and the heat actually means something (how recent), which is exactly the line between "themed" and "cringe". The open layer leads with the big number, so you get Bento's recruiter punch without the generic grid.

Content homework first: every role needs one quantified line. Barclays (45 min to near instant) and AMEU (6,000+, +120%) are great. BlackRock, Outlier and Finexus need a number or a concrete outcome: how many evaluations, which system, what it replaced. Also grab the missing logos (Outlier, Finexus, Petaling Utama Motor) so fewer monograms show.
