03 · Arctic Island · New purpose

Kill the logo wall. Give it a local.

You're right, a tech stack wall is dated. Nobody hires off a grid of logos. Below are four things Arctic could become, then four monsters who could live there. Click a scene to drive it.

Where it's at

src/islands/tech.js shows about 54 full-colour logos in 7 groups, from Python to Bambu Lab, plus a glacier photo.

What's off

A logo says "touched it once" and "shipped with it for years" the same way. It's the least personal panel in the whole world, and every dev site has one.

The move

The stack's real job is answering "can he do X?" Answer that properly, with a character, and the logos can retire as tags.

Part 1 · What lives here now

Four new purposes

Each one replaces the tech stack in a different way. All demos use real facts from your repo. Anything that needs your input is marked as a placeholder.

A · Ask Mochi

An NPC who answers questions about you, grounded in your real CV. Tap a question or type your own. Every answer links to the island it came from.

Try: "does he know react?" or "kubernetes"

Why it works

It does the tech stack's job, properly. "Can he do Kubernetes?" gets a straight answer plus where you used it. It's the most memorable thing in the world, and it quietly proves you can ship AI features, which matters in 2026.

HCI + accessibility

Suggested chips mean zero typing is needed. Answers are short and always cite a source island, so trust is earned. The log is an aria-live region. Typing never fires game keys. Reduced motion shows answers instantly.

How it'd be built

A Vercel function api/ask.mjs, same pattern as api/jukebox.mjs, calling Claude (claude-haiku-5-5) with your CV, projects and roles in the system prompt. Short answers in brand voice, refuses off-topic, rate limited per IP. Common questions are cached. This demo's offline matcher becomes the fallback.

Watch out

It can make things up, so ground it tightly and make "ask Eric directly" the default for anything unknown. Keep it cheap with Haiku, caching and a daily cap. Add a topic guard so it doesn't become a free chatbot.

Why it works

Your stack list says ROS 2, Bambu Lab, xTool, Fusion 360. That's rare for a CS grad and right now it's buried as logos. Physical builds make you stand out from every other web dev, and Mountain stays for polished client work.

HCI + accessibility

Simple filter chips, cards in a scannable grid, status words (not just colour) for shipped, WIP, failed. Every card has the same anatomy so eyes learn it once.

How it'd be built

Swap GROUPS in tech.js for a BUILDS array: title, line, status, photo, tools. Same panel, same reveal. Photos go in public/lab/. The frost refraction is pure CSS.

Watch out

Only works if you have photos of real builds. Four great ones beat twelve mediocre ones. "Failed gloriously" is charming once, not five times.

Why it works

Very native to a game. Lets a recruiter skim your best moments in one screen, and the progress bars (7 Wonders, countries) make it feel alive instead of finished.

HCI + accessibility

One row anatomy, icon plus text, so colour never carries meaning alone. Progress has numbers, not only bars. Locked trophies say how to unlock, so it's a hint not a wall.

How it'd be built

A small ACHIEVEMENTS array. "Islands found" reads the same visited set MapOverlay already tracks. "Countries" reads travel-stats.json. Cheap to build, around 120 lines.

Watch out

It overlaps Lava (experience) a lot. Achievement titles can tip into cringe fast, so keep them plain. Can read as bragging if the list is padded.

Why it works

Makes the world feel lived in. Return visitors see something new. Linking "Listening" to your Jukebox loop ties two islands together nicely.

HCI + accessibility

Bento cards with icon plus label. The clock uses tabular numbers so it doesn't jitter. "Last updated" is honest about freshness.

How it'd be built

One NOW object in a data file, Intl.DateTimeFormat for the London clock. Under 80 lines. Could even read from a JSON in Blob so you update it without a deploy.

Watch out

A stale Now page is worse than none. If you won't update it monthly, skip it. It's also the least "wow" of the four.

My pick

Arctic becomes Ask Mochi. Pip hides in the snow.

Ask Mochi is the boldest move on this page and the one that actually replaces what the tech stack was for. A recruiter asks "has he used Kubernetes?" and gets a real answer with a source, from a fluffy yeti, inside a game. Nobody forgets that. It also shows you can ship a grounded AI feature end to end, which is worth more than any logo.

Mochi is the monster: round, soft, friendly, readable at a distance and easy to build from an icosahedron. Put Pip in a snow hole nearby as an easter egg: catch it three times and it unlocks a secret. That's where the Trophy Cave idea can live later, as a tiny reward, not a whole panel.

The logos don't vanish, they retire: tools become tags on Mountain's projects and things Mochi can talk about. If you'd rather not run an AI feature, The Lab is the strong fallback, as long as you have photos of real builds.
