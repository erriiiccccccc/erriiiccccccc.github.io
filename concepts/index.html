Where the game differs from this bible

Principle 07, "entrances under 600ms"The shells take 0.5 to 1.2 seconds to open and about half that to close. They run at 60 fps, but they're longer than the bible says.
Island numbersThis bible numbers Meadow 01, Mountain 02, Arctic 03, Lava 04, Desert 05, Pirate 06. The game numbers them in walking order round the planet: Meadow 01, Lava 02, Desert 03, Arctic 04, Mountain 05, Pirate 06.

Who it's for

Two players, two clocks

Every decision in this bible serves both at once. If a feature helps one and blocks the other, it doesn't ship.

30 sec

The recruiter

Has a stack of applicants and a coffee going cold. Initial résumé screens are commonly measured in seconds, and a personal site is rarely the deciding filter. They need to know what you do, how good you are and how to reach you, fast.

  • Panel titles say what, not where
  • Résumé one click from anywhere
  • Every role and project leads with its outcome
  • The Short Version, if they won't play at all
5 min

The player

A friend, a fellow dev, a recruiter who got curious. They came to poke at things. They remember you because something surprised them, then they tell someone else.

  • Islands to find, a counter that matters
  • A character who reacts, emotes and talks
  • Locals: Mochi, Pip, the Jukebox, the Doodle Wall
  • Small delights: true skies, paper planes
The rules

Eight principles

These replace the v1 "AAA principles" list and keep everything good from it (game vocabulary, Options not Settings, found not visited).

01

Facts never wait for fun.

The game is the wrapper, never the lock. Anyone can get the essentials without playing.

Do"Projects" as the title, a Short Version link on the loader
Don'tHide your résumé behind three islands
02

Biome through light, not props.

Islands are told apart by colour temperature, light, motion and texture. Never by costume.

DoHeat as recency on Lava, a true sky on Desert
Don'tRivets, parchment, flags, cartoon fire
03

The world never disappears.

Opening an island keeps the planet and your character in view. You went somewhere, you didn't leave.

DoSide stage, character idling beside the panel
Don'tA black scrim that ends the game
04

One shell, six souls.

Same header, keys, close and tokens everywhere, so it's learned once. The body is where each island is different.

Do"02 / 06 · Mountain Island" then "Projects", every time
Don'tSix different close buttons
05

Lead with the outcome.

Every role and project opens with what changed because of you, in one line, ideally with a number.

Do"45 min to near instant"
Don't"Aladdin engineering operations and systems"
06

Answer in 100ms.

Every press gets a visible reaction within a tenth of a second. Panels open within a second.

DoThe keycap sinks the instant you press E
Don'tA pause before anything happens
07

Motion is a flavour, never a gate.

One beat per event, entrances under 600ms, content readable at every frame. Reduced motion swaps movement for fades.

DoA 520ms frost-in on Arctic
Don'tContent that appears after a two-second show
08

Found, not visited.

Finding an island is an event with a moment, a number and a reward for finishing.

Do"Island found · 3 / 6" title card, a secret at 6 / 6
Don'tA counter nobody notices
New · the express lane

The Short Version

The single highest-value addition in this whole bible. One plain, fast, readable page with everything that matters, reachable from the loader and the HUD. It protects you from the biggest risk of a 3D world: someone who won't wait, can't run WebGL, uses a screen reader, or is on a weak phone. It also gives search engines something to read.

Motion

One rhythm, everywhere

Three curves, five durations. Click a curve to feel it.

Tap feedback
120ms
Hover, toggle
180ms

280ms
Panels

Camera, world

  • Enter with ease-out, exit with ease-in, exit takes about 65% of the enter time.
  • One beat per event. If two things animate, one leads and one follows 40ms later.
  • Never block input. Every animation can be interrupted by the next key press.
  • Under reduced motion (OS setting or Options), every movement becomes a 200ms fade. Information stays, motion goes.
Sound

A world in one key

Each island owns a note from G major pentatonic, so any two islands sound good together. Events reuse the island's note: arriving plays it, diving in plays it with a fifth, finding all six plays the whole chord. Runs on the shared AudioEngine the Jukebox already uses, and stays silent until Sound is on in Options.

What the research says

The evidence behind the picks

Where these rules come from. I've flagged where a source is weaker than it's usually quoted as.

Recruiter behaviour

First looks are measured in seconds

Ladders' 2018 eye-tracking study put the average initial résumé screen at about 7.4 seconds, with recruiters jumping to titles, companies and dates.

Its method has been questioned and other estimates vary widely, so read it as "fast", not as a precise number.

So wePut titles that say what, outcomes first, and the Short Version one click away.
Source: HR Dive on the Ladders study
Hiring managers

The site is rarely the filter

A survey of 60+ hiring managers found most would look at a candidate's site, but it rarely changed the outcome. Résumé, GitHub and LinkedIn came first. Reviewers value the problem, the approach and the tradeoffs over polish.

Small 2021 survey, but it matches what most hiring guides say.

So weMake the world a bonus that's fast to read, and add a "what I did, what happened" line to every project.
Source: Profy.dev survey
Information foraging

People follow the scent

Pirolli and Card's information foraging theory (Xerox PARC), popularised by Nielsen Norman Group: people choose paths by cues that predict value. Weak or misleading scent makes them give up.

So wePut a teaser in the prompt ("9 projects · 5 playable demos") and use plain section names.
Source: NN/g, information scent
Game UI

Diegetic where it's about a place

Fagerholt and Lorentzon's "Beyond the HUD" (Chalmers, 2009) splits game UI by whether it lives in the world and in the fiction. Later work found plain HUD elements can be less disruptive for critical info.

So wePin the prompt to the island (diegetic), but keep the found counter, controls and Options as clean HUD.
Sources: Beyond the HUD (PDF) · Game Developer
Response time

0.1s, 1s, 10s

From Miller (1968) via Nielsen: under 0.1 seconds feels instant, under 1 second keeps your flow, past 10 seconds you lose attention and need progress plus a way out.

So weReact to presses within 100ms, open panels within a second, and give the world.glb loader (15 MB after compression) a progress bar and the Short Version escape.
Source: NN/g, response time limits
Game feel

Juice, in moderation

Jonasson and Purho's "Juice It or Lose It" (GDC Europe 2012) layers particles, sound and motion onto a plain game until it feels great. Critics point out that too much juice can cost immersion.

So weGive each event one beat of juice (the Charge press, the paper plane), never a fireworks show.
Source: GDC Vault
Accessibility

Big enough to hit

WCAG 2.2 adds 2.5.8 (AA): pointer targets at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels, or spaced so they don't crowd. The stricter 2.5.5 (AAA) asks for 44 by 44.

So weUse 44px as the minimum for every control in the world UI, and make whole tags and cards clickable.
Source: W3C, Understanding 2.5.8
Accessibility

Reduce motion, don't remove meaning

The prefers-reduced-motion setting exists for people with vestibular disorders and motion sensitivity. Guidance is to swap big movement for fades, avoid autoplay, and offer an in-app toggle too.

So weHonour both the OS setting and your existing Options toggle in every new animation, and never autoplay video under reduced motion.
Source: web.dev, animation and motion
