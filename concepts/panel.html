System · What happens after E

Stop floating. Start diving.

Right now pressing E fades the world and a card floats up in the middle. It works, but it feels like a website modal, not a place you went into. Four ways to make the dive feel spatial, plus the shared shell every island would use.

Where it's at

#world-panel in overlay.css: a radial scrim dims the planet, a glass card scales from 0.96 to 1 in the centre. Same entrance for all six islands.

What's off

The card comes from nowhere, so there's no link between the island you stood on and what opened. The world vanishes behind a dark scrim. To see another island you close, walk, press E again.

The move

Open from the island, keep the world in view, and let people hop between islands without closing.

Four ways to dive in

Pick a dive

Press "Dive in" (or click the scene and press E). Close with Esc or the × button. Placeholder content keeps the focus on the motion.

A · Iris

The panel opens as a circle expanding out of the island's position on screen, the content settles in one block at a time, and closing shrinks it back into the island.

Spatial continuity

Impact
Effort
Risk

Why it works

Your eye follows the circle from the island to the content, so the brain files it as "I went into that island". Closing back into it tells you where you are again. It's the cheapest way to fix "it comes from nowhere".

HCI + accessibility

Motion from the trigger is a core platform rule (modals should grow from what opened them). Exit is faster than entry (380 vs 640ms) so closing feels snappy. Reduced motion uses a plain fade.

How it'd be built

Project the island's position to screen coords (you already compute island dirs in main.js), set them as CSS variables, animate clip-path: circle(). Around 30 lines on top of the current panel.

Watch out

clip-path on a large blurred glass element can drop frames on weak GPUs. Fall back to opacity on the Low quality preset.

B · Side Stage

The camera slides the island and your character to the left third, and the panel slides in from the right. The world never disappears, your character idles next to the content. On phones it's a bottom sheet you can drag down.

Try it narrow too: drag the handle

Impact
Effort
Risk

Why it works

It's how games do shops and menus: the character stays on screen, so you never feel "taken to a website". It's the single biggest thing that makes Eric's World! feel like a game instead of a site with a 3D intro.

HCI + accessibility

The world staying visible keeps context, so people never feel lost. The sheet is a standard pattern (side sheet on desktop, bottom sheet on mobile) with a clear close and Esc. Focus moves into the sheet and back to the world on close.

How it'd be built

Camera.js gets a "framed" mode: offset the orbit target so the island sits left of centre (you already have follow, preview, detail lerps). The panel becomes a right-anchored sheet. No full-screen scrim, so no blur over live WebGL.

Watch out

Content gets about 60% of the width, so Mountain's video hub may want the full Chapter width. Keep rendering the world at a lower pixel ratio while a panel is open to save battery.

C · Chapter

A full-screen takeover with the world softly blurred behind, and a slim rail of all six islands down the side. ← and → hop to the next island without closing, with a directional slide.

Open it, then press ← →

Impact
Effort
Risk

Why it works

Turns six separate modals into one journey. Recruiters in a hurry can read everything in a row with the arrow keys, players can still walk. The rail doubles as a progress tracker.

HCI + accessibility

Forward slides left, back slides right, so direction always matches the key. The current island is marked by a bar and colour, not colour alone. The rail is real buttons.

How it'd be built

The panel keeps its shell and swaps the body via takePanelBody() from content.js (already cached). Behind it, the camera warps to the next island using the Island Map warp you already have.

Watch out

Hides the world the most, so it's the least "game" of the four. Blur over live WebGL is expensive: use a cheap darkened frame instead on Low quality.

D · Biome Entrances

Same layout, but each island arrives with its own one-beat signature: Meadow blooms, Mountain rises, Arctic frosts into focus, Lava cools from a glow, Desert shimmers through heat haze, Pirate washes in like a tide.

Pick an island below the scene

Impact
Effort
Risk

Why it works

It's "all islands look different" applied to motion, the cheapest way to give each island a soul. Each one lasts under 600ms, a flavour, not a show.

HCI + accessibility

Content is readable at the end of every entrance, never waiting on a long animation. Reduced motion swaps all six for one simple fade.

How it'd be built

Six small Web Animations API keyframe sets keyed by island variant, plus one SVG turbulence filter for Desert. Lives next to the existing wp--variant classes.

Watch out

Six different motions can feel inconsistent if any runs long. Same duration and easing family for all, only the "material" changes.

My pick

Side Stage, opened with the Iris, flavoured by the biome, with hop arrows.

Side Stage is the layout because keeping the world and your character on screen is what makes this a game. The Iris is how it opens, so the panel visibly comes out of the island. Each island adds its biome beat on top, kept under 600ms. And the shell header gets the ‹ › hop buttons from Chapter, so people can read all six in a row.

On phones it's a bottom sheet with a drag handle. Mountain is the one exception that can widen to full Chapter width, because video needs room.

The shared shell

One shell, six souls

Every island panel uses the same header grammar so people learn it once. What changes per island is the body layout, the accent and the entrance. This is the shell all the island concepts use.
