System · The approach prompt

Press E, but make it feel like a game.

The capsule that says "[E] Explore" works, no complaints. But it's the moment every single visitor sees six times, so it should be the most polished thing in the world. Five takes below. In each scene: click it, walk closer with W, press E.

Where it's at

src/ui/UI.js builds #island-popup: a glass capsule pinned bottom-centre with the island icon, name, section, and an E keycap with a pulse ring.

What's off

It's a HUD sticker: same spot, same look, no matter where the island is. It tells you the name but not why you'd go in. And it says "Explore" while the brand bible says the verb is "dive in".

The move

Celebrate the find, anchor the prompt to the island, give a reason to press, make pressing feel physical.

Five takes on the prompt

Pick a prompt

Use the coloured dots in each scene to switch island. W or ↑ walks closer, S or ↓ steps back, E dives in.

A · Area Title

The first time you step onto an island, its name fills the screen like a new area in a game, the found counter ticks up, then it shrinks into the compact prompt. After that, just the prompt.

Reset to see it again

Impact
Effort
Risk

Why it works

"Found" is a brand word, and right now finding an island has no moment. A title card makes it an event and makes the 6 / 6 counter matter. Games have done this forever because it works.

HCI + accessibility

Plays once per island, never blocks input, skippable by walking on. Under reduced motion it becomes a simple fade. Text is huge and high contrast.

How it'd be built

A DOM overlay triggered from the same proximity check that shows the popup today, gated by MapOverlay's visited set. CSS keyframes only. About 60 lines.

Watch out

Must never play twice for the same island, or it turns into an annoyance fast. Keep it under 2.5 seconds.

B · World Tag

The prompt lives in the world: a tag floating above the island with a thin line down to a glowing ring. It moves with the island, grows as you get closer, and clings to the screen edge with an arrow when the island is off-screen.

← → or drag to pan the camera

Impact
Effort
Risk

Why it works

Diegetic UI (living in the world) feels like a real game, a fixed HUD sticker feels like a website. It also answers "which island is this about?" with zero thought, because the line points at it.

HCI + accessibility

The edge arrow helps people find islands, a wayfinding win. The whole tag is the click target. Scaling stops at a readable minimum so text never gets tiny.

How it'd be built

Project the island's anchor point with Vector3.project(camera) each frame and move a DOM element there (cheap). Clamp to the viewport and swap to the arrow when off-screen.

Watch out

Needs smoothing so it doesn't jitter when the camera moves. Must not cover the character. Two tags on screen at once needs a rule (nearest wins).

C · Peek

The prompt carries a teaser of what's inside: "9 projects, 5 playable demos" with three thumbnails, or "now: Teaching Assistant". It opens up as you arrive, so people know why it's worth pressing.

Try Mountain, then Pirate

Impact
Effort
Risk

Why it works

Information scent: people press more when they can smell what's behind the door. A label says where you are, a teaser says why you'd go in. Probably the biggest lift in press-through of all five.

HCI + accessibility

Opens on arrival, not on hover, so it works on touch. One line of text plus optional thumbnails keeps it scannable while walking.

How it'd be built

Add a teaser field to each island in src/data/content.js. Thumbnails reuse the project posters already in public/projects/. Grid-rows trick for the expand.

Watch out

Teasers must stay true and current ("now: ..." goes stale). Keep it to one line, it's a sign not a panel.

D · Charge

Pressing E feels physical: the keycap sinks, a ring fills in 160ms, sparks burst in the island's colour and the prompt flies into the island as the dive starts. Optional soft chime.

Turn sound on for the chime

Impact
Effort
Risk

Why it works

Feedback within 100ms makes things feel responsive, and "juice" (sparks, sound, motion that points where you're going) makes them feel good. The prompt flying into the island bridges straight into the dive.

HCI + accessibility

No hold-to-confirm: holding is for destructive actions, opening content should be instant. The whole thing is 160ms, never a wait. Sound is off by default.

How it'd be built

CSS for the keycap and ring, a few spark elements animated with the Web Animations API, a two-note chime through the shared AudioEngine the Jukebox already uses.

Watch out

Pure polish: it doesn't help anyone decide to press. Do it last, after the prompt carries real information.

E · The Combo

All four, layered so each does one job. First find: the Area Title. After: the World Tag, whose second line is the Peek teaser. On press: the Charge. On phones the whole tag is one big "tap to dive in" button.

Walk up to a new island first

Impact
Effort
Risk

Why it works

Each layer answers one question in order: "where am I?" (title), "which thing?" (tag), "why go in?" (peek), "did it work?" (charge). Nothing competes, because they happen at different moments.

HCI + accessibility

Desktop shows the E keycap, touch shows "tap to dive in" and the entire tag is the target (well over 44px). Reduced motion keeps all the information and drops the movement.

How it'd be built

Replace #island-popup in UI.js with a WorldTag class fed by the camera projection, add the teaser field, the title overlay, and the press juice. Roughly 250 lines total, no new libraries.

Watch out

Build in order (peek, tag, title, charge) and ship each one, so you're never stuck with half a system.

My pick

The Combo, built in this order: Peek, Tag, Title, Charge.

Peek is the one that changes behaviour (more people press when they know what's inside), so it goes first. The World Tag is what makes it feel like a game instead of a website. The Area Title gives "found" the moment it deserves. Charge is the cherry.

Copy fix right now, even before the redesign: change the keycap label from "Explore" to "dive in", and the mobile one to "tap to dive in". The brand bible already says so, the UI just never caught up.
