02 · Mountain Island · Projects

Your work, front and centre.

Mountain is where people decide if you're good. Right now your work shares the screen with stock photos. Here are three ways to make it the only thing that matters. Click a tab or press 1, 2, 3, then click into a scene to drive it.

Where it's at

src/islands/projects.js lays out a sideways collage: 9 projects interleaved with 9 Unsplash travel photos (Fuji, Santorini, Iceland). Videos play in smallish frames.

What's off

The stock photos aren't yours, so people can't tell which tiles are work. Everything is the same size, so nothing is the hero. Your 5 real demo videos never get the full screen.

The move

One project at a time, big and moving. The demo is the hero, everything else is one key press away.

Three ways to show the work

Why it works

Your demos are the strongest thing you have, and right now they're thumbnails. Here one is always playing, huge. People get what you build in five seconds, then flick through like a game menu, which is the whole point of the world.

HCI + accessibility

One focus at a time, so no choice paralysis from 18 tiles. Arrows, click, or swipe the rail. Text sits on a heavy gradient so it reads over any video. Reduced motion shows posters, no autoplay. "04 / 09" tells you where you are.

How it'd be built

Swap the collage in projectsLayout.js for two stacked video layers that crossfade. Reuse the data in projectsData.js minus ART_RAW. The rail is a scroll-snap row. Arrow keys are free because movement pauses while a panel is open. Around 250 lines.

Watch out

Needs a demo or a real poster for every project, otherwise covers carry 4 of the 9. Preload only the next video so bandwidth stays sane. Videos must stay muted to autoplay.

    Why it works

    The fastest answer to "show me everything". Big type does the heavy lifting, which reads confident and senior. The floating preview gives every row a moment of delight without clutter.

    HCI + accessibility

    Huge rows are huge targets. ↑ ↓ and Enter work, filters cut 9 to 2 in one click. The preview never holds info the row doesn't, so nothing is hover-only. On phones there's no cursor, so rows expand an inline preview instead.

    How it'd be built

    A list of buttons plus one floating media element reused for every row (one video, swap the src). Position lerps in requestAnimationFrame. The drawer is a sheet inside the panel. Around 180 lines.

    Watch out

    This look is common on designer sites. Polished, but less unique to your world. With 9 projects the list can feel short unless the type is really big.

    Why it works

    The most unique of the three and the only one that belongs to Mountain. Your work becomes a climb from 2022 to now. Growth gets shown, not claimed. Topo lines are the modern outdoor-brand look, zero costume.

    HCI + accessibility

    ← → walks one waypoint at a time, so it's never a hunt. Waypoints are real buttons (Tab and Enter work). The card shows on select, not on hover. Contours are decoration and hidden from screen readers.

    How it'd be built

    Contours generated once as SVG (wobbly circles), the trail is one path, the walker uses getPointAtLength. Later you could trace the real mountain_island silhouette from world.glb for the contours. Around 220 lines.

    Watch out

    Slowest to scan, and the map eats space media could use. Seven of nine projects are 2024, so the climb bunches up. Gets crowded past about 12 projects.

    My pick

    Ship the Game Hub. Keep The Index as a list toggle.

    Your demos are your best asset, and the Game Hub is the only option that puts one front and centre, moving, the second the panel opens. It's also the most native to a game: you pick a project like you pick a save file. Recruiters get it in five seconds, players flick through for fun.

    Add a small list icon in the panel header that flips to The Index for people who want everything at once. Same data, two views. Keep Topo Trail in your back pocket, maybe as a tiny altimeter in the hub later.

    Before you build: drop the Unsplash photos from Mountain. They aren't your work and they make people wonder what you built. Your real photos belong on Pirate Ship. Record demos or real posters for Eric's World!, ELK Pipeline, Blender and Ada Hack, and write one line per project on what you did and what happened. Tech logos die on Arctic and live here as tags.
